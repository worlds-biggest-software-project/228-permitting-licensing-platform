## Relevant Industry Standards or Protocols

- **IBC (International Building Code)** — model building code adopted by most US jurisdictions; drives permit type taxonomies and inspection checklists in permitting platforms
- **ICC (International Code Council) Digital Codes** — digital publication and integration of building and fire codes; permitting systems must align code references to inspection workflows
- **ADA / Section 504** — accessibility requirements for government-facing online permit portals and constituent-facing interfaces
- **Section 508** — US federal accessibility mandate applicable to government technology procurement
- **GML / CityGML** — geographic markup language standards for parcel and land use data integration; relevant for permitting systems with GIS layers
- **Open311** — standard API for government service requests; used to integrate permitting status into broader constituent service portals
- **BLDS (Building & Land Development Specification)** — open data standard for permit data exchange enabling transparency and third-party integration

## Market Research

**Market Size:** The permit software market was valued at approximately $266 million in the narrow government-sector definition for 2026, growing to $401 million by 2035 at a 4.7% CAGR. Broader definitions of the permitting and licensing software market (including enterprise and industrial) place the 2025 value at $3.8–4.8 billion, projected to reach $8.6–10.6 billion by 2034 at roughly 9–10% CAGR. Tyler Technologies holds approximately 14% global market share.

**Funding:** Tyler Technologies is publicly traded (NASDAQ: TYL) with multi-billion dollar market capitalisation. OpenGov was acquired by Cox Enterprises. Camino Technologies and Cloudpermit have raised venture funding targeting modernisation of legacy government systems.

**Pricing Landscape:** Enterprise platforms (Tyler, Oracle, Accela) range from $100,000 to $1 million+ per year for large municipalities. Mid-market platforms (OpenGov, Cloudpermit, SmartGov) run $20,000–$150,000/year. Cloud-based deployment accounts for 58.7% of the market. More than 68% of enterprises have shifted from manual workflows to automated platforms.

**Key Buyer Personas:** City and county building departments processing construction permits; local government licensing divisions issuing business and professional licences; state agencies managing environmental and transportation permits; construction contractors seeking faster permit turnaround; planning departments managing land use applications.

**Notable Trends:** Digital permit submission is now standard expectation; over 68% of government agencies have adopted automated platforms. Mobile inspection tools with photo documentation and offline capability are a key differentiator. AI-assisted plan review is emerging to address inspector resource constraints. AI and GIS integration for zoning analysis and parcel lookup is an active development area.

## AI-Native Opportunity

- Automated plan review pre-screening: Computer vision and AI models could analyse uploaded construction drawings for common code compliance issues (egress widths, occupancy loads, structural basics) before human plan reviewers engage, reducing review cycles from weeks to days.
- Intelligent application routing and completeness checking: AI could assess incoming permit applications for completeness, identify missing documents, and route applications to the correct review queue without human triaging.
- Predictive inspection scheduling optimisation: ML models could analyse inspector workloads, geographic routing, permit complexity, and historical inspection duration to generate optimised daily inspection schedules, reducing inspector travel time.
- Natural-language permit eligibility assistant: Conversational AI on the applicant portal could answer zoning questions, explain permit requirements for specific project types, and guide applicants through the correct application pathway.
- Anomaly detection for licensing compliance: AI could monitor licence renewal pipelines, flag businesses operating with expired or lapsed licences, and generate automated enforcement notifications, improving compliance without manual tracking.
